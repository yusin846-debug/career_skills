# Opportunity decision framework

## 1. Verification gate

Before scoring a live role, verify from official sources where possible:

- exact title and team;
- country, city, and office or remote expectations;
- employment type;
- requisition identifier when available;
- whether the Apply action is live;
- posting or update date;
- public closing date, if any;
- mandatory versus preferred experience;
- language, degree, license, travel, and work-authorization conditions.

If no fixed public deadline exists, say **rolling / no fixed public deadline** and set a separate internal deadline. Never convert an internal working date into a claimed company deadline.

## 2. Source-conflict rule

Use this priority order:

1. official job detail page with a working Apply flow;
2. official employer job list;
3. official employer team or location page;
4. a reputable mirror only for metadata absent from official sources;
5. search snippets only as leads.

Do not declare a role closed from one stale detail URL when the official job list and Apply flow remain active. Report the conflict and inspect the current application state. The prior Snowflake SDR mistake is the standing warning: live-state triangulation is mandatory.

## 3. Hard blockers

A hard blocker can override a high numerical score. Test for:

- a mandatory degree, language, license, location, work authorization, or travel condition that is not met;
- required technical or industry depth that cannot be evidenced;
- materially different scope from the target career lane;
- a confirmed closed application;
- a compensation or employment condition the user has rejected.

Do not treat a preferred qualification as mandatory. Do not dismiss a mandatory qualification as mere recruiter wish-list language.

## 4. Weighted score

Score each dimension from 0 to 5 and calculate `score / 5 × weight`.

| Dimension | Weight | Question |
|---|---:|---|
| Functional fit | 25 | Does the actual weekly work match the target activity mix? |
| Evidence strength | 20 | Can the user prove the required behavior and outcomes? |
| Transition friction | 15 | How little explanation, retraining, or title-reset risk is required? |
| Organizational learning | 15 | Will the role teach robust methods, systems, and operating cadence? |
| Long-term exchange value | 15 | Does it improve future mobility into valuable B2B tech roles? |
| Application realism | 10 | Are level, requirements, location, timing, and competition credible? |

Interpretation:

- **75–100:** priority application;
- **65–74:** selective application with a tailored narrative;
- **55–64:** stretch application only when upside justifies the work;
- **below 55:** deprioritize.

State the major assumptions behind the score. False precision is worse than an honest range.

## 5. Required risk diagnosis

Every verdict must address:

1. **Functional fit:** what the person will actually do, not the job title.
2. **Overqualification:** why the level reset will not cause disengagement or rapid exit.
3. **Experience gap:** which direct industry, tool, or operating context is absent.
4. **Retention:** why this is a durable specialization rather than a temporary brand move.
5. **Evidence:** which claims will survive resume screening and behavioral interview probing.

## 6. Career exchange test

A role is valuable when it purchases several of these assets:

- a repeatable sales or customer-success methodology;
- measurable ownership of pipeline, revenue, adoption, retention, or forecast;
- enterprise customer exposure;
- credible proximity to data, cloud, AI, or CRM platforms;
- internal or external career mobility;
- strong manager coaching and feedback;
- experience with mature systems and cross-functional cadence.

A recognizable logo alone is not enough. Penalize roles that reset seniority without returning transferable methods or market value.

## 7. Lane-specific evidence

| Lane | Lead evidence | Primary concern to answer |
|---|---|---|
| Customer Success | onboarding, adoption, renewal, expansion, commercial objections | direct SaaS product and portfolio experience |
| Sales Ops / RevOps | pipeline, forecast, variance, CRM, action planning | analytical tools and operating-scale evidence |
| SDR / BDR | prospect research, outreach, qualification, learning speed | level reset, outbound stamina, quota motivation |
| BD / Partnerships | partner problems, offers, contracts, portfolio growth | domain relevance and repeatability |
| Enterprise Sales | account strategy, stakeholders, economics, closing | true enterprise scope and forecast ownership |
| Enablement / CRM-AX | process mapping, adoption, training, requirements translation | depth of hands-on platform delivery |

## 8. Verdict format

Use a compact decision table when comparing opportunities:

| Item | Decision |
|---|---|
| Verdict | Apply now / Build then apply / Stretch / Deprioritize / Do not apply |
| Score | Total plus decisive assumptions |
| Best fit | One or two concrete reasons |
| Biggest risk | The risk most likely to cause rejection |
| Lane | The single resume and portfolio lane |
| Deadline | Official date or rolling status, plus internal working date |
| Next action | The smallest action that changes the outcome |
